Direct or Indirect?

Name: _____________________________    Class: ____________    Date: ____________

Word check
  • Direct pressure — Someone clearly asks, tells, or dares you to do something.
  • Indirect pressure — No one asks you — you feel you should because others are doing it.

Circle whether each example is direct or indirect pressure.

  1. You copy your older cousin because you look up to them.
    DirectIndirect
  2. “Go on, just try it — everyone's doing it.”
    DirectIndirect
  3. “I dare you to take a puff.”
    DirectIndirect
  4. You see the popular kids drinking and want to fit in.
    DirectIndirect
  5. “If you were really my friend, you'd have one.”
    DirectIndirect
  6. No one offers you anything, but you feel left out for not joining in.
    DirectIndirect
  7. Someone hands you a drink and says, “Here, take a sip.”
    DirectIndirect
  8. Everyone at the table is vaping, so you feel like you should too.
    DirectIndirect

Answer key

#StatementAnswerWhy
1You copy your older cousin because you look up to them.IndirectCopying someone you admire is indirect pressure.
2“Go on, just try it — everyone's doing it.”DirectSomeone is directly telling you to do it.
3“I dare you to take a puff.”DirectA dare is a direct challenge aimed straight at you.
4You see the popular kids drinking and want to fit in.IndirectThe pressure comes from wanting to belong, not from a request.
5“If you were really my friend, you'd have one.”DirectThey're directly pressuring you by using your friendship.
6No one offers you anything, but you feel left out for not joining in.IndirectFeeling left out is a form of indirect pressure.
7Someone hands you a drink and says, “Here, take a sip.”DirectThey're directly offering it and asking you to.
8Everyone at the table is vaping, so you feel like you should too.IndirectNo one asked — you feel pressure to copy the group.
